Call of Duty PC settings for more FPS and less input lag

Which settings to change, why, and which ones to leave alone. Tested on the game’s own settings file.

The three settings that matter most for input lag

Before chasing FPS, fix latency. These three settings decide how fast your mouse movement reaches the screen.

SettingValueWhy
NVIDIA ReflexOn + BoostKeeps the render queue empty: the frame you see is the one you just aimed. On AMD, use Anti-Lag 2.
V-Sync (gameplay and menus)OffV-Sync waits for the screen and adds a full frame of delay. With G-SYNC, cap your FPS a little under the refresh rate instead.
Frame generation (DLSS / FSR / XeSS)Off in multiplayerGenerated frames look smooth but are not real inputs: they add latency. Keep it for the campaign.

Graphics settings that cost FPS for little visual gain

These effects eat GPU time and barely help you spot enemies. Lowering them is the cleanest FPS gain in the game.

SettingValueWhy
Shadow qualityVery lowOne of the most expensive settings, and shadows rarely help visibility.
Particle qualityVery lowExplosions and smoke cost a lot in big fights, exactly when you need FPS.
Volumetric effectsLowFog and light shafts look nice but hide players and drain the GPU.
Screen-space reflectionsOffCostly, and reflections are distracting in fast fights.
Ray tracingOffThe biggest FPS cost of all, for no competitive benefit.
Ambient occlusion / lightingOffDarkens corners; turning it off can even make players easier to see.
TessellationOffExtra geometry detail on terrain that you will not notice mid-fight.
Motion blur (world and weapon)OffBlur hides detail when you turn. Always off for competitive play.
Depth of fieldOffBlurs the background while aiming. Off for a clear picture.

Settings to leave alone

  • Resolution and render scale: keep native resolution, or use DLSS “Quality” / “Balanced” if you need more FPS. Lowering the render scale makes enemies blurry.
  • Texture quality: it uses video memory, not GPU power. Keep it high if your card has 8 GB or more.
  • Field of view: a personal choice. Wider shows more but makes targets smaller.

Video memory scale and frame limit

Video memory scale (VRAM): 80 to 90%. Above 90%, the card runs out of memory during map changes and the game stutters. Below 80%, textures stream in late.

Frame limit: with G-SYNC or FreeSync, cap a few FPS under your refresh rate (for example 141 on a 144 Hz screen) and keep Reflex on. Without adaptive sync, leave it unlimited or well above the refresh rate. In-game, the limit goes up to 300 FPS.

Where Call of Duty stores its settings

The latest Call of Duty games keep their settings in a text file under %LOCALAPPDATA%\Activision\Call of Duty\players, named after the game (for example s.1.0.cod25.txt0 for Black Ops 7). Close the game completely before editing it: Call of Duty rewrites the file when it quits. Game updates often reset it, so check your settings after each big patch.

Measure, don’t guess. Play one match before changing anything and one after, in the same mode, and compare average FPS and 1% low. A difference under 3% is normal match-to-match variation.

Questions

Can changing Call of Duty settings get me banned?

No. These are the game’s own options, written in its own settings file. Nothing is injected into the game.

Should I turn on DLSS frame generation in multiplayer?

No. Frame generation adds input lag. Use it only in the campaign, and keep DLSS Super Resolution if you need more FPS.

Why did my settings come back after an update?

Call of Duty often resets its settings file after big patches. Reapply your settings, or let KarmaaOptimizer detect the reset and reapply them in one click.

What frame limit should I use?

With G-SYNC or FreeSync: a few FPS under your refresh rate. Without it: unlimited, or well above your refresh rate.
