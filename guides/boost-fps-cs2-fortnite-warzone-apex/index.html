How to boost FPS in CS2, Fortnite, Warzone and Apex Legends

Updated November 22, 2026 · 5 min read

Before touching any setting, find out what limits your FPS: the graphics card or the processor. The answer decides which settings help and which do nothing. Then here are, game by game, the options that cost the most for the least visible benefit.

Step 1: find out what limits your FPS

Open a performance overlay while playing (NVIDIA App overlay with Alt + R, AMD Software overlay with Ctrl + Shift + O, or MSI Afterburner) and watch GPU usage:

  • GPU at 95–100%: you are GPU-limited. Lowering resolution, upscaling (DLSS, FSR, XeSS) and heavy graphics settings will raise FPS.
  • GPU well under 90% while FPS is not capped: you are CPU-limited. Lowering graphics barely helps. What helps: closing background apps, a performance power plan, RAM at its rated speed (XMP/EXPO), and lowering CPU-heavy settings (view distance, physics, player count effects).

Esports games like CS2 and Fortnite in Performance mode are very often CPU-limited on good GPUs. Battle royales like Warzone are often GPU-limited at 1440p.

Always compare the same scene, and look at 1% lows, not just the average: they show stutter.

Step 2: the Windows basics (all games)

  • Latest graphics driver from NVIDIA, AMD or Intel.
  • Correct refresh rate selected in Windows, Game Mode on, power mode on Best performance.
  • Background recording off (Settings → Gaming → Captures), useless overlays closed.
  • RAM running at its rated speed (XMP/EXPO in the BIOS).
  • Game installed on an SSD.

Details in our Windows 11 settings guide. For latency, also turn on NVIDIA Reflex (or AMD Anti-Lag 2) in every game below that offers it: see our input lag guide.

Game menus change with updates. Option names below may move or be renamed; the logic stays the same.

Counter-Strike 2

CS2 is mostly CPU-limited on good GPUs. Settings worth checking in Settings → Video → Advanced Video:

  • Boost Player Contrast: Enabled. Small cost, makes players easier to see.
  • Wait for Vertical Sync: Disabled.
  • NVIDIA Reflex Low Latency: Enabled.
  • Multisampling Anti-Aliasing Mode: CMAA2 or 2x MSAA is a good balance; 8x MSAA costs a lot.
  • Global Shadow Quality: Medium or High lets you see player shadows better; Low gives the most FPS. Test what you prefer.
  • Model / Texture Detail, Shader Detail, Particle Detail: Low to Medium. Texture detail depends mostly on VRAM, not FPS.
  • Ambient Occlusion: Disabled. High Dynamic Range: Performance.
  • FidelityFX Super Resolution: Disabled unless you are really GPU-limited; it makes the image softer.

Launch options: many old ones (-novid, -high, -threads) do nothing useful in CS2 or can hurt. You can leave launch options empty. To cap your frame rate, use the in-game max FPS setting or the console command fps_max.

Fortnite

In Settings → Graphics, the first choice is the Rendering Mode:

  • Performance – Lower Graphical Fidelity: the lightest mode, designed for high FPS. It is the usual choice for competitive play and for modest PCs.
  • DirectX 12: needed for the newest visual features (Nanite, Lumen). On a strong PC, it can run well if you turn those features off.

In DirectX 11 / DirectX 12 modes, the most expensive settings are:

  • Nanite virtualized geometry: Off.
  • Global Illumination and Reflections (Lumen): Off.
  • Hardware Ray Tracing: Off.
  • Shadows: Off or Low.
  • Effects and Post Processing: Low.
  • View Distance: Medium or Far (too low hides players far away).
  • Textures: depends on your VRAM, not your FPS, as long as you have enough memory.

Also set NVIDIA Reflex Low Latency to On (or On + Boost), V-Sync off and Frame Rate Limit to your refresh rate or slightly below with G-Sync/FreeSync. Fortnite also recompiles its shaders after updates: the first matches after a patch can stutter, then it settles.

Call of Duty: Warzone

Warzone is heavy on the GPU and on VRAM. In Settings → Graphics:

  • Display Mode: Fullscreen Exclusive.
  • NVIDIA Reflex Low Latency: On + Boost, or On.
  • Upscaling / Sharpening: DLSS, FSR or XeSS in Quality or Balanced mode if you are GPU-limited. It is often the biggest FPS gain at 1440p.
  • VRAM Scale Target: around 80–90%. Too high can cause stutter when VRAM runs out.
  • Texture Resolution: Normal or Low on 8 GB cards.
  • Shadow Quality, Screen Space Reflections, Ambient Occlusion, Volumetric Quality: Low or Off.
  • Particle Quality: Low. Depth of Field, Motion Blur, Film Grain: Off.
  • On-Demand Texture Streaming: turning it off avoids downloading textures during matches; it can reduce stutter on slow connections, with slightly blurrier textures.

Let shader preloading finish in the menu before your first match after an update or driver change. Playing while it compiles causes stutter.

Apex Legends

Apex can be CPU-limited in fights with many effects. In Settings → Video:

  • Display Mode: Full Screen. V-Sync: Disabled.
  • NVIDIA Reflex: Enabled or Enabled + Boost.
  • Adaptive Resolution FPS Target: 0 (off), to keep a sharp image.
  • Anti-aliasing: None or TSAA.
  • Texture Streaming Budget: match your VRAM (it does not cost FPS while it fits).
  • Ambient Occlusion Quality: Disabled. Sun Shadow Coverage and Sun Shadow Detail: Low. Spot Shadow Detail: Disabled. Volumetric Lighting and Dynamic Spot Shadows: Disabled.
  • Model Detail, Effects Detail, Ragdolls: Low. Impact Marks: Disabled.

Apex caps FPS by default in some setups. To change the cap, add the launch option +fps_max 0 (unlimited) or a value like +fps_max 237 in Steam or the EA app (game properties → advanced launch options).

Step 3: measure, then stop

  1. Pick one repeatable scene per game (a practice range, a replay, a custom match).
  2. Note average FPS and 1% lows before changing anything.
  3. Change a group of settings, play the same scene, note again.
  4. Keep what helps, revert what does nothing visible.

Past a certain point, more FPS does not help if your monitor cannot show it and your latency is already low. A stable frame rate with good 1% lows feels better than a high but unstable average.

Common myths

  • “This launch option gives +100 FPS”: most viral launch options are outdated or do nothing.
  • Setting the game to “Realtime” priority in Task Manager: it can freeze input and audio. “High” at most, and usually not needed.
  • Disabling all Windows services: no measurable FPS gain, real risk of breaking anti-cheat or updates.
