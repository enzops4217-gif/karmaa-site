How to reduce input lag on Windows 11

Free guideUpdated November 22, 20266 min read

Input lag is the delay between your click and the moment the result shows up on screen. On Windows 11 most of it comes from a handful of settings in your game, your graphics driver and Windows itself. Here is how to fix them by hand, in the order that matters most.

What input lag really is

When you click, the signal goes through a chain: the mouse sends it over USB, Windows passes it to the game, the game simulates the frame, the graphics card renders it, then the monitor scans it out. Total latency (often called system latency or click-to-photon) is the sum of all these steps. On a decent gaming PC it is usually between 15 and 40 ms. Most of it comes from the render queue (frames waiting for the GPU) and from V-Sync, not from the mouse or Windows.

That is why the order below matters: fix the big contributors first, then the small ones.

1. Measure before you change anything

Without a measurement, you are guessing. Pick a scene you can repeat (a training map, a replay, an empty custom match) and note:

  • your average FPS and your 1% lows (they show stutter better than the average);
  • the latency number if your game shows one: many games with NVIDIA Reflex have a “Render latency” or “PC latency” stat in their performance overlay;
  • on NVIDIA, the free FrameView tool logs PC latency in Reflex games.

Change one setting at a time and measure again in the same scene. Results vary a lot from one PC to another.

2. Turn on NVIDIA Reflex or AMD Anti-Lag

This is the single biggest win in most competitive games, because it keeps the render queue empty when your GPU is the bottleneck.

  • NVIDIA Reflex: in the game’s graphics settings, set it to On. On + Boost also keeps the GPU clocks high; it helps a little when you are CPU-limited, at the cost of power use.
  • Games without Reflex (NVIDIA): open NVIDIA Control Panel → Manage 3D settings → Low Latency Mode → Ultra (DirectX 11 and older only; it does nothing in DirectX 12 or Vulkan games).
  • AMD: open AMD Software → Gaming → Graphics → turn on Radeon Anti-Lag. In supported games, Anti-Lag 2 is set in the game itself.

3. Fix V-Sync and cap your frame rate properly

Classic V-Sync can add a full frame or more of delay. What to do depends on your monitor:

With G-Sync or FreeSync (variable refresh rate)

  1. Turn on G-Sync / FreeSync in the driver and on the monitor.
  2. Turn off V-Sync in the game.
  3. Turn on V-Sync in NVIDIA Control Panel (Manage 3D settings → Vertical sync → On). With G-Sync this does not add the usual V-Sync lag; it only prevents tearing at the top of the range.
  4. Cap your FPS a few frames below your refresh rate (for example 237 on a 240 Hz screen, 141 on 144 Hz). Reflex does this cap automatically when G-Sync and V-Sync are both on.

Without variable refresh rate

Leave V-Sync off everywhere. A higher frame rate than your refresh rate still lowers latency, at the cost of some tearing. If your FPS swings a lot, a stable cap (set in-game or in the driver) that your PC can hold most of the time often feels better than an unstable uncapped frame rate.

4. Use the right display mode

On Windows 11, borderless windowed is no longer the latency trap it used to be, as long as the game uses the modern “flip” presentation model:

  1. Open Settings → System → Display → Graphics.
  2. Click Advanced graphics settings (or Change default graphics settings on older builds).
  3. Turn on Optimizations for windowed games.
  4. On the same page, try Hardware-accelerated GPU scheduling. It needs a restart. It helps on some PCs and does nothing on others, so measure both ways.

If a game offers true Fullscreen (exclusive), it is still a safe choice. Avoid running the game in a regular window with the taskbar visible.

5. Check your refresh rate and power plan

A surprising number of players run a 144 or 240 Hz monitor at 60 Hz without knowing it.

  1. Go to Settings → System → Display → Advanced display.
  2. Under Choose a refresh rate, pick the highest value. If it is missing, check your cable (use DisplayPort or a recent HDMI cable) and the port on the GPU, not on the motherboard.
  3. Go to Settings → System → Power & battery and set Power mode to Best performance. On desktops you can also pick the High performance plan in the classic Control Panel → Power Options.

6. Fix the mouse side

  • Enhance pointer precision: Control Panel → Mouse → Pointer Options → uncheck it. It is mouse acceleration; most games using raw input ignore it, but it affects menus and some older games.
  • Polling rate: use at least 1000 Hz in your mouse software. Higher rates (2000 to 8000 Hz) shave off fractions of a millisecond but cost CPU time. Read our polling rate guide before going to 8000 Hz.
  • USB port: plug the mouse (or its wireless dongle) into a rear port on the motherboard, not a front panel, hub or monitor port. Details in our USB port guide.

7. Clean up background load

Background programs rarely add direct input lag, but they cause frame time spikes that feel like lag.

  • Turn off background recording: Settings → Gaming → Captures → turn off Record what happened. Turn off instant replay in the NVIDIA or AMD overlay if you do not use it.
  • Close overlays you do not need (Discord, launchers, RGB tools, monitoring apps).
  • Check Task Manager → Startup apps and disable what you do not need at boot.
  • If you feel random micro-stutters, run the free LatencyMon for a few minutes. If it points at a driver (network, audio, storage), update that driver from the manufacturer’s site first.

Keep your graphics driver up to date, but use a clean install only when something is broken. A new driver can change latency in a game, so measure again after updating.

Settings that do not help (or can hurt)

  • Random registry “latency tweaks” found in videos: many do nothing on Windows 11, and some (timer or interrupt changes) can make the PC unstable. If you try one, write down the original value first.
  • Disabling every Windows service: it can break updates, the Microsoft Store, audio or anti-cheat services, for no measurable gain.
  • Max pre-rendered frames tweaks in DirectX 12 games: the driver setting does not apply there. Use Reflex or Anti-Lag 2 instead.

Quick checklist

  1. Reflex On (or Low Latency Mode Ultra / Anti-Lag).
  2. In-game V-Sync off; with G-Sync/FreeSync: driver V-Sync on and FPS cap a few frames under the refresh rate.
  3. Fullscreen, or borderless with Optimizations for windowed games on.
  4. Real refresh rate selected, power mode on Best performance.
  5. Pointer precision off, 1000 Hz or more, rear USB port.
  6. Recording and useless overlays off.
  7. Measure again in the same scene.
