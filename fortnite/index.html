Fortnite PC settings: Performance mode and max FPS

Performance mode or DirectX 12, which graphics to lower, and the one setting you should keep high.

Performance mode or DirectX 12?

Performance mode is a lightweight renderer made for competitive play. It gives the highest and most stable FPS on most PCs, especially when the processor is the limit, and the lowest input lag. DirectX 12 looks better and supports more effects, but costs more and can stutter while shaders compile after an update.

For competitive play, start with Performance mode. Switch to DirectX 12 only if you have a strong PC and care more about visuals.

Frame limit

With G-SYNC or FreeSync, cap a few FPS under your refresh rate. Without adaptive sync, set the limit above your refresh rate (240 or 360 for example) or unlimited. A stable frame rate feels better than a higher one that keeps dropping.

Where Fortnite stores its settings

Fortnite keeps its settings in %LOCALAPPDATA%\FortniteGame\Saved\Config\WindowsClient\GameUserSettings.ini. Many players set the file to read-only after tuning it, so the game cannot reset it. If you edit it, close Fortnite completely first.

Measure, don’t guess. Compare a match before and after in the same mode. Look at the 1% low as much as the average: that is what you feel as stutter.

Questions

Is Performance mode better for FPS?

On most PCs, yes. It gives the highest and most stable FPS and the lowest input lag, especially when the processor limits the game.

Should I lower view distance?

No. View distance decides how far you see players. Lower shadows, effects and post-processing instead.

Why does Fortnite stutter after an update?

In DirectX 12 the game compiles new shaders after updates. It settles after a few matches. Performance mode is much less affected.

Can editing GameUserSettings.ini get me banned?

No. It is the game’s own settings file. Nothing is injected into Fortnite.
