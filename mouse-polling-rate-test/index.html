What is polling rate?

Polling rate is how often your mouse sends its position to the PC. At 125 Hz it reports every 8 ms, at 1000 Hz every 1 ms, at 8000 Hz every 0.125 ms. A higher rate means smoother tracking and slightly less delay between your hand and the screen.

Which polling rate should I use?

  • 1000 Hz: the safe choice for nearly everyone. Smooth, low delay, light on the processor.
  • 2000 to 4000 Hz: a small extra gain on high refresh rate screens (240 Hz and above) if your PC has CPU headroom.
  • 8000 Hz: the smoothest tracking, but it uses more CPU and some games stutter with it. Test it in your own games.

Why does the result jump around?

A mouse only sends a report when it moves. Slow movements send fewer reports, so the value drops. Move quickly and steadily in circles for the real rate. A wireless mouse in power-saving mode can also report less often.

Questions

My 4000 Hz mouse shows 1000 Hz. Is it broken?

Probably not. Browsers group mouse events and cap the value around 1000 Hz. Use a native tool such as the one in KarmaaOptimizer to measure higher rates.

Does polling rate affect FPS?

Very high rates (4000-8000 Hz) use more processor time, which can lower FPS in games that are limited by the CPU. 1000 Hz has no measurable cost.

How do I change my polling rate?

In your mouse manufacturer’s software (Logitech G HUB, Razer Synapse, etc.), or with a button under some mice.
